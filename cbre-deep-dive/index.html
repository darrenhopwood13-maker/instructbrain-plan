CBRE EMEA Technical - deep dive

What the technical division does, what it is currently pointed at, the reporting it already sells, where instructBrain fits, and where CBRE actually publishes templates.

Prepared forDal Hopwood
AccountCBRE · EMEA Technical
SourcesCBRE's own published record + RICS
CirculationInternal - not for external release

In two lines. CBRE EMEA Technical is the arm that inspects buildings, prices their condition and liabilities, and monitors other people's developments - then hands it over as reports. In the UK and Ireland it sits inside Design & Build → Project & Programme Management, alongside Technical Due Diligence, Dilapidations, Development Monitoring, Engineering and Façade Consultancy, Buildings & Systems and Cost Advisory.

It is not the facilities-management business. FM is GWS - a separate, far larger machine (UK Local alone: 7,000+ staff, 850+ contracts, $2bn). Technical is the advice FM and the transaction teams lean on. That distinction is the one to hold in the room.

The sentence worth remembering from their own page. "A single point of contact is our critical link in delivering consistency and quality across the UK and Europe." Note the scope - UK and Europe, not global. Any pitch that assumes a global problem is answering a question they did not ask.

Compared against the report types verified in the instructBrain repository and the capability dossier IB-CAP-001 (9 Oct 2026).

Two design rules that matter more in this account than in any other. instructBrain routes to Not assessed rather than guessing a pass - and where the evidence does not support a judgement, a finding must be resolved by a person before the report can be issued. For a due-diligence or dilapidations practice, that is not a feature, it is the reason the document is worth anything in a negotiation. Lead with it here.

04 Their templates online - what actually exists

Dal's question was whether their templates are published. The honest answer is: the supplier templates are, the client report templates are not.

mySupplier training guides Supplier onboarding walkthroughs - how to register, how to invoice, how to comply. found
Supplier sample documents CBRE publishes a sample-documents page through its supplier enrolment portal. found
Client-facing report templates
(TDD, schedule of condition, dilapidations)		 Not published, and there is no leak to find. Those documents follow the RICS professional standards and CBRE's internal practice groups. Their site confirms the governance: CBRE runs a UK & Ireland Technical Due Diligence Best Practice Group covering process, procedure, governance and quality. does not exist publicly

What this means for us. Do not hunt for a CBRE template to copy - it is not out there, and implying we have one would be a credibility risk. The published authority is RICS, and the rules there matter: technical due diligence is a global RICS professional standard; dilapidations is England & Wales only and must not be described as global; "Building surveys and technical due diligence of commercial property" was archived on 5 Dec 2022 and must not be cited as current; and there is no standalone RICS Schedules of Condition standard. Our four sample reports already sit in the right shape - schedule of condition, electrical installation, finding and remedial schedule, manual photographic record.

05 Where the fit is, and what will be probed

The fit, in their language

  • "A single point of contact... consistency across the UK and Europe." Their stated quality mechanism is consistency, and consistency at scale is exactly what template-driven, standards-bounded capture gives you. This is the strongest opening line in the account.
  • "Leaving no open issues and no questions unanswered." Their TDD page's own promise. Our Not-assessed gate and per-item actions with named owners are how that promise is enforced rather than asserted.
  • Development monitoring is a monthly report treadmill. Every monitored scheme generates a visit report, month after month, across a £4bn book. That is the highest-volume report type in the division and the easiest to put through a photo-to-report engine.
  • Mobilisation and handover. The property inventory engine - rooms, item tables, meters and keys - is facilities-management mobilisation work that CBRE already bills for. It is the least obvious feature on our site and the most relevant one here.
  • Cross-border portfolios. Thirty languages with English as the record copy answers an EMEA portfolio problem directly.

The four things they will probe

ProbePosition, honestly
"Is this an Ellis capability, or a supplier product?" CBRE already runs Ellis AI in-house - publicly described as its AI platform - plus agent work with Google and McKinsey. This question decides the whole shape of the deal and must be answered before architecture is discussed. Ask it first, do not assume the answer.
Security & enterprise readiness SSO/SAML: not present. SOC 2 / ISO 27001: no evidence. Data residency: UK/EU choice not documented. CAFM/IWMS connectors: none. Say so plainly and scope it as work. Note the market context: no UK condition-survey tool publishes SSO at all, and nobody in the class offers on-prem or customer-selectable residency - so this is a category gap, not just ours.
Supplier Code of Conduct It requires audit rights (desktop, on-site, records review), data-protection compliance, breach notification regardless of the supplier's own assessment, and records that are created and maintained, never altered or omitted. Our audit-log table and frozen issued versions line up with the last of those. The first three are process, not product.
Brand CBRE's Standards of Business Conduct prohibit using their name or logo in public promotion without written exemption. Anything client-facing needs that permission in writing first.

06 Questions to ask

  1. Is the technical reporting problem here an Ellis AI problem or a supplier opportunity? Who decides?
  2. How many monitoring visits and condition surveys does EMEA Technical produce in a year, and what is one worth to produce?
  3. Which is the bigger pain: the cost of producing a report, the time it takes, or the consistency of the output between surveyors and countries?
  4. Who owns the template set today, and how does a change to it get released across the region?
  5. What would a novice surveyor need on site to produce a document a director would sign without editing?
  6. On security: is SSO required at proposal stage, or scoped as an implementation condition?

07 What a good outcome is

Good: one named report type, agreed as a paid pilot, produced through instructBrain and compared side by side with the current output. Development monitoring or a schedule of condition is the natural first one - high volume, well understood, and low brand risk.

To avoid: being drawn into an architecture conversation before the Ellis question is answered; promising SSO, certifications or residency; and describing dilapidations as a global product line. All three are cheap to avoid and expensive to recover from.
