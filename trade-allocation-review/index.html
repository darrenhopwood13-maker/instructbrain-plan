Headline

The brief's eight risks all point at something real. Six are confirmed against the code and the live database, two are partially confirmed - the guard exists but cannot check a concept that does not yet exist.

The machinery is in better shape than the brief assumes. What is genuinely missing is one layer: the mapping between a trade and the company appointed to do it. Everything else - the directory, the per-trade extract, the send log, the frozen published copy, the database-level confidentiality block - is built and working today.

3. Risk verification

# Risk from the brief Verdict Evidence
1 Company and trade mapping conflated; duplicated contacts drift Confirmed project_directory holds trade and company_name on the same row. No company entity exists. One firm across five trades = five rows repeating the same name and contact
2 Multiple subcontractors for one trade ambiguous; first match taken Confirmed distribution-data.ts: directoryRows.find(candidate => candidate.trade === group.key) then contacts.find(is_primary) ?? contacts[0]. Findings are grouped by trade, so a second firm on the same trade never appears as its own row and nothing warns
3 Case-insensitive in the UI, exact in distribution Confirmed groupKeyOf trims but does not case-fold; the directory lookup is ===. The picker's dedupe is case-insensitive, and known-trades.ts comments claim identity is case-insensitive "matching how allocation groups trades" - the comment does not match the grouping code
4 AI uses template trades, not the project's register Confirmed No project trade register exists. The suggestion vocabulary is tradesOf(snapshot) - the report template's own list
5 Free-text trade entry creates drift Confirmed, with live evidence Stored trades observed in the database included whole sentences, e.g. "Lifting equipment inspection body (LOLER competent person) with the plant hirer for the MEWP". Nothing in a directory can ever match that, so those items fall to the fallback. These are live in the weekly register drafts FC-LI-001 and FC-WE-001 in the Demonstration account, so the evidence is checkable today rather than taken on trust
6 Package boundaries and exclusions unmodelled Confirmed No scope, zone, floor, block or exclusion columns exist on any mapping. project_directory.notes is the only free text. Findings carry a location, and distribution can group by area, but that is finding-side, not appointment-side
7 Unmapped or ambiguous items need visible triage; the fallback must not look like an answer Partially confirmed Verified live: the unassigned row is labelled "No trade assigned" with "(fallback recipient)", and blocked rows print plain-word reasons ("Groundworks has no contact with an email address in the project directory"). The review screen carries unsure_trade and no_trade_suggested attention reasons. Not verified: whether an extract delivered through the fallback is itself marked as unallocated - that needs a fixture and a send
8 Bulk confirmation needs strict limits Partially confirmed bulkTradeEligible requires no trade assigned, a non-empty suggestion and confidence at or above BULK_TRADE_CONFIRM_THRESHOLD (0.80 - the test pins 0.8 eligible, 0.79 not). bulk-accept.ts separates what one press may settle from what a person must decide, and ties its blocked flag to the publish gate. But it cannot check "trade is in the project register" or "package is unambiguous", because neither concept exists

The brief's checklist of "existing concepts observed" is accurate in every particular: the template supplies the trade list; AI suggestion and human confirmation are stored in separate columns (ai_suggested_trade, assigned_trade); directory rows carry trade, company, active state and a primary contact; distribution groups by assigned trade and uses the primary contact; a project-level fallback exists; confidential findings are blocked at database level; and the one-press confirmation uses a real 80% threshold.

4. Recommended user journey

  1. Project setup - one screen. Tick the trades this job actually has, from a standard library. Project-specific trades can be added deliberately, never typed by accident. Mark one firm as covering Groundworks, Drainage, Reinforced concrete and Below-ground utilities, and the project knows the structure rather than a list of labels.
  2. Add each subcontractor once. One company record, many trades. Save the whole thing as a template so the next project starts in one click.
  3. AI suggests, from the project's list only. Confidence and reasoning shown; it abstains when uncertain, and it never names a company as a fact.
  4. A person confirms the trade, and where two packages could apply, chooses the package. Ambiguity is never resolved by taking the first match.
  5. Pre-flight before anything sends - unassigned items, missing mappings, ambiguous packages, missing or invalid contacts, excluded scope, unconfirmed findings, confidential findings.
  6. One extract per subcontractor, covering every trade that firm holds, with each line still showing its trade so their own crews know. Not five emails to the same firm.
  7. Send deliberately. A person presses the button. Every send is recorded against the report and the issued version.
  8. Issued reports stay frozen. Changing an appointment afterwards never rewrites an issued report or the PDF an existing share link serves.

5. Data model proposal - smallest safe change

Keep everything that works. Add the missing layer beside it.

New tables

  • subcontractors - organisation-scoped company record: id, organisation_id, name, notes, is_active.
  • project_trades - the project's active register: project_id, trade_id, label, is_active, sort_order.
  • trade_packages - the mapping: project_id, trade_id, subcontractor_id, zone_or_floor, scope_note, exclusion_note, is_active, effective_from, effective_to.
  • trade_library - canonical trades: id, label, aliases. Seed it from the trades the survey definitions already carry in survey_type_definitions rather than inventing a new vocabulary.
  • findings.confirmed_package_id - nullable foreign key. Keep assigned_trade exactly as it is.

Migration and backfill

  1. Derive project_trades from the distinct trades already present per project (findings plus directory rows).
  2. Derive subcontractors from distinct company_name within each organisation.
  3. Derive trade_packages from each existing project_directory row - one package per row.
  4. Leave project_directory in place and keep it working; move the UI to the new tables first, then retire the old rows. No downtime, no rewrite of any issued report or version document.

Security

New tables take the same policy pattern as the existing ones: is_org_member() for reads, has_org_role(..., ARRAY['owner','admin','surveyor','supervisor']) for writes, owner/admin for deletes, WITH CHECK on insert, and a project_org()-style resolver. Verified as the house pattern across fourteen existing tables.

Compatibility

Issued reports, version documents and existing share links are untouched by any of this. The findings_locked_when_issued trigger already prevents post-issue edits; mapping changes are project-level and must never reach a version document.

7. Prioritised plan

First, before anything else

  1. Clear the two typecheck errors on main, and make test + lint + typecheck the gate every future change is measured against. Two lines; it should not sit red.

Must fix before this is promised to a client

  1. Package model with boundaries and exclusions - risks 1, 2 and 6. This is the whole point of the brief.
  2. Project trade register, closed AI vocabulary and stable trade ids - risks 3, 4 and 5.
  3. One extract per subcontractor rather than per trade.
  4. Pre-flight completeness, and bulk confirmation that refuses an ambiguous package or an unregistered trade - risks 7 and 8.
  5. The fallback labelled as triage, never as an answer.

Next, once the above is settled

  1. Directory and package setup UI, with the template/CSV path moved onto the new model.
  2. Effective dating on packages, so a change of appointment is recorded rather than overwritten.
  3. The Directory navigation item shown whenever the trade layer is on, with a line explaining it when it is off.

Not doing, deliberately - per the brief, no trade scoring, no subcontractor league tables, no performance rankings. The tool documents and distributes observations; it does not rate companies.

8. Meeting-ready summary

What is already true today, and can be shown

  • A project can hold its trades, with a company and a named contact against each, and that setup can be saved as a template so the next project starts in one click.
  • Each item can be attributed to a trade, with the AI's suggestion, its confidence and its reasoning shown, and a person confirming before anything is treated as final.
  • Each trade receives only its own items - verified live: the extract carries the line "This extract lists only the items attributed to Groundworks on this report. It is not the full report."
  • Nothing sends automatically. Every send is one deliberate press and is recorded.
  • Published reports and their links serve a frozen copy. Reopening a report does not disturb a link.
  • Findings involving a person cannot reach a subcontractor - blocked in the database, not just hidden in the interface.
  • Menus, screens and the published PDF are one coherent piece of software, not a prototype.

What is proposed, and would be built

  • A project-level map of trades to subcontractors: twenty trades, five firms, one firm covering groundworks, drainage, civils and concrete - set up once, reused on every project.
  • The AI choosing only from that approved register, never inventing a trade that no firm covers.
  • Where one trade is split between firms by floor, zone or block, the split is recorded and a person resolves it - ambiguity is shown, never silently guessed.
  • One extract per firm, not one per trade.
  • A pre-flight check before any send, so missing mappings and missing contacts are visible before anything leaves the building.

Two honest limits for the room

  • Where a project has no mapping yet, the tool will say so. It does not know who is responsible, and it will not guess.
  • Nothing in this rates a subcontractor. It records what was observed and who was appointed.

Questions worth asking CBRE - the brief lists the right ones: whether responsibility is managed by trade, package, or zone; whether one firm covers several trades and one trade splits across firms; where scope boundaries and exclusions are recorded; who may confirm responsibility; what should happen when an issue cannot be allocated confidently; and what audit evidence is required when an issue is allocated, changed or distributed.
