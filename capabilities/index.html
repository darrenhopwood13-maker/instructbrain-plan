One line: instructBrain turns a site walk into an issued, citation-backed report — photographs taken on site are drafted into findings, a person confirms every one, and the whole thing is published as a PDF that cannot change underneath you afterwards.

1What it is, and who it is for

Someone walks a building with a phone. Each photograph is filed against the element, floor or room it belongs to. The product drafts what the photograph shows into plain findings — what is there, what it means, what to do about it — and pulls the reference it engages, if any, off a fixed list. A person then confirms, corrects or bins each item. What survives that gate is published as an issued PDF, with a reference, a version number and a permanent record of what was issued and when.

It is built for the people who currently produce that paperwork by hand: site managers and clerks of works (schedules of condition, snagging and remediation), health and safety consultants (compliance registers, H&S documentation), property and asset managers (property inventories, condition records), and fit-out and M&E teams (installation condition, commissioning records).

The shape of itProject → report → photographs → draft findings → a person confirms each one → issue → distribute. Every stage is a screen, and every stage leaves a record behind it.
Where it runsA browser at instructbrain.com: on a laptop in the office, and on a phone or tablet on site. The on-site cockpit installs to a home screen and opens full-screen from a QR code.
What comes outA paginated PDF, brand-consistent, with the report reference on every page, photographs with pins marking what each finding refers to, and a cover carrying project, client, address, reference, date, status and item count.

2The eleven report types

These are the eleven templates in the product today, named as the product names them.

#ReportWhat it produces
1Schedule of ConditionItem-by-item condition grading. Each item carries its own photographs, a graded condition, the action required, and the reference the visible condition engages.
2Site conditionThe site walk recorded and issued: what was seen, where, and what follows from it.
3Property inventoryA room-by-room inventory of what is present and in what state — fixtures, fittings, finishes, services, and meter readings where they are recorded.
4Electrical installation conditionCondition of the electrical installation: observations, classifications and the standard each one engages.
5Mechanical & HVAC installationPlant, ventilation and heating condition and installation quality, against the acceptance and commissioning standards for each system.
6Fit-out & finishes qualityQuality of the delivered fit-out by element: finishes, joinery, tiling, glazing, envelope — each item with severity and the responsible trade.
7Damp, mould & water ingressWhere water is getting in and what it has done: location, extent, probable cause and the remedial route.
8Weatherproofing membrane surveyMembrane and weathertightness condition, element by element, with the remedial action for each.
9Finding identification & remedial scheduleThe snagging and remediation list: every finding with its severity (cosmetic, workmanship, performance, compliance, structural or safety), its responsible trade, the action required, and the reference it engages.
10Photo condition recordA photographic record of condition, filed and issued — the evidence, without the narrative layer.
11Manual photographic reportNo AI anywhere in the output. For the surveys where the report must be built by hand, the machine layer is off and the pages carry only the item reference and the capture date and time.

Every finding on every one of those templates carries the same spine: status, severity, trade, location, the photograph (with a pin marking the relevant area), the likely cause, the action required, and a reference — or deliberately no reference at all.

4The four rules that make the output defensible

Anyone can take a photograph and type a sentence. These are the rules that decide whether the resulting document can be relied on.

1A person confirms every finding — the machine drafts nothing that issues itself
In practiceFindings arrive as drafts. Each one is confirmed, corrected or removed by a person, and the product will not publish while anything is still unconfirmed or unassessed. There is a named gate for it, and the report simply does not issue through it.
2Citations come off a fixed list — never invented
In practiceThe instruction the product gives itself is to select the single most relevant reference, and only from the supplied list — and never to invent, infer or guess a regulation, clause, section, table or paragraph number. Where nothing on the list applies, it cites nothing rather than reaching for something that sounds right.
3An issued version is frozen
In practicePublishing locks the report. Reopening it does not edit what was issued — it starts a new version, and the published one is kept exactly as it was published, with its version number and date.
4There is a manual path with no AI in it at all
In practiceFor work where a machine must not touch the output, that template runs with the automated layer off: photographs, item reference, capture date and time, and nothing generated. It is a mode of the product, not a workaround.

6The weekly compliance register

Alongside the reports, a project carries a weekly compliance register: the checks that were carried out, week by week, rolled up into one register that shows what was checked, when, and by whom, against a fixed window of weeks. It is the screen an H&S consultant or a principal contractor's auditor asks for, and it is built from the same evidence as the reports rather than typed up afterwards.

The register is read from the product's own compliance module and check definitions. I have not signed in and driven a live register run, so the flows above are described from the product's code and its issued documents, not from a session on the running system.

7From the site to the issued PDF

#StepWhat happens
1CapturePhotographs are taken on site and filed against the element, room or location they belong to. Location values stamp onto each photograph and become the location the report prints.
2DraftThe product writes what the photograph shows into a finding: description, condition or severity, probable cause where the image supports it, and the action required.
3ConfirmA person works through every item, correcting the language, setting the trade, and confirming, amending or deleting it. Nothing unconfirmed can publish.
4IssueThe report is published: it gets its reference and version, the document is assembled, and the published version is frozen.
5DistributeThe issued PDF is sent to the recipients on the project, or handed over as a link. Nothing leaves without a deliberate send.
6KeepEvery issued version stays available with its number and date, so a superseded report can be produced years later exactly as it was issued.

8Everything around the report

Field cockpitA cut-down on-site screen at its own address, reachable by scanning a QR code and installable to a phone's home screen so it opens full-screen in a coat pocket. It captures; the office screens do the rest.
DirectoryTrades and contacts per project, with the trades that appear on findings kept against the people responsible, and a fallback recipient held on the project so distribution never depends on one person's inbox.
Trades and recipientsReports go to the recipient list on the project. Individual links exist for sharing a report with a person or a trade without giving them the account.
Languages30 languages, including right-to-left for Arabic and Hebrew, with English always kept as the record copy. Translated views switch off editing so the issued record and the translation cannot drift apart.
Organisation brandingReports carry the customer's identity alongside the product mark, so the document goes out as their paperwork. Issued and manual-mode output keeps the fixed product identity rather than adopting an organisation colour.
ReferencesReport references are allocated by the database in a fixed pattern (for example SOC-2026-10-001, SNG-2026-10-006) so two reports cannot claim the same one. Reports created before that existed keep their hand-typed reference, with a note saying so.

9Data and access

Who sees whatEvery screen sits behind a sign-in and an organisation membership. A report belongs to an organisation; a user sees the organisations they are a member of, and nothing else.
The recordIssued versions, compliance registers and photographs are retained with their dates. Registers can be archived rather than deleted, so the record of a week's checks survives finishing the job.
DeletionDeleting a project is a deliberate act that takes its reports, photographs and registers with it, and it says so before it does it.
AdminAn organisation owner manages the organisation, its directory and its people; a platform administrator role exists above that for support.

11What it does not do

Stated on the document, because it travels further than this conversation does.

  • It is not a drawing or modelling tool. There is no CAD, no BIM and no estimating in it. It documents what is there; it does not design what should be.
  • The machine drafts language, it does not decide engineering. Severity, trade and sign-off are set and confirmed by a person. The product is built so an unreviewed item cannot be published.
  • It will not invent a citation. Where the fixed list holds nothing that fits, the finding carries no reference. That is deliberate, and it means some findings cite nothing at all.
  • It sends nothing by itself. Distribution is an action a person takes. There is no automatic outbound email from a report.
  • Verification limits on this dossier. Every claim here was read from the product's own source code and its issued sample documents on 9 October 2026. I did not sign in and walk the running application, so screen behaviour is described from the code and from issued output rather than from a supervised session. Figures quoted (11 report types, 30 languages, 12 Approved Documents, 6 standards, 6 HSE or statutory instruments) are counts read from the source that day.
  • The projects in the samples are invented. Riverside Court and Halloway Cross Estates are demonstration projects, not client work.
