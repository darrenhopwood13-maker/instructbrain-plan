3Job 1 — trade allocation as a switch

You asked for the whole feature to be an option: enable or disable. Here is what “the whole feature” actually is, because it is wider than one screen.

1What is welded in todayMEASURED5 surfaces
ReviewThe Trade card and the bulk Confirm all trades action appear whenever the template says trades are required. The template flag is frozen into each survey definition, so it is per-template and not something you can turn off from the screen.
Publish gatePublishing is blocked while any finding has no trade, on templates that require one. That gate lives in issueBlockers() in src/lib/report/document.ts and is read by the report screen and the publish button.
DirectoriesTwo screens: your organisation directory in Settings, and a project directory per project, each with contacts. 857 lines in the project one.
DistributionThe send screen groups items by trade and fires one email per trade with its own extract and its own live link. With no trades assigned, it collapses to one email — which is what happened on the test send.
Trade portalA public token page (/trade/<token>) where a subbie marks their own items done. One has ever been issued.
2What the switch would mean, exactlyPROPOSED7 changes
Off means
  • No trade card and no trade gate in the review screen — the finding card is shorter and nothing asks you for a trade.
  • Publishing no longer blocked on a missing trade. Everything else about the gate is unchanged.
  • The organisation directory and project directory screens are hidden from navigation.
  • Distribution shows one whole-report row: no per-trade grouping, no per-trade extracts.
  • The per-trade extract route and the trade portal go quiet; existing links are honoured read-only, nothing breaks for a subbie holding one.
  • The PDF drops its per-trade appendix. The report itself is untouched.
  • Emails still work end to end — the whole-report send is unaffected.
Off never meansDeleting anything. Directory rows, contacts and assigned trades stay in the database and come back the moment the switch goes on. No published version is altered, and an already-published report always reads the same as the day it was published.
Where it livesA setting in Settings → Organisation, plus a per-report override, so “this job doesn’t need trades” is a switch on the day and not a global decision. Two new database columns, one migration, additive only.
Why that shapeMost of the machinery already exists as a per-template flag. What is missing is a control a person can reach. Per-template alone (today’s behaviour) means you cannot turn it off without a code change; organisation-only means one report cannot differ from the next.

Decision 1 — how far does the switch reach?

A. Organisation setting plus a per-report override. MY RECOMMENDATION
Default on for existing accounts so nothing changes for anyone until they switch it off, one control on the report for a job that does not need trades. Two columns, one migration, the honest cost is about a session including tests.
B. Per report only. Smaller and safer: the switch travels with the job. But you would still see the directory screens and the trade plumbing on every other report.
C. Organisation only. Simplest mental model, one switch for the account. The cost is that a one-off custom report cannot opt out while the account stays on.
D. Leave it as it is. Nothing changes. Honest answer: the trade layer is nearly unused, so this keeps costing every report a screen and a gate for the customers who do not need it.

4Job 2 — one press to confirm everything

This is the one you called the big thing, and I agree: it is not a button problem, it is a “how many decisions does a person make per report” problem.

1How it works today — five ways to say yesMEASURED1106-line screen
The five
  • Confirm on a finding — accepts the AI’s wording and status, one item at a time.
  • Confirm grade on the condition card — a second, separate press for the same item.
  • Confirm assignment on the trade card — a third press for the same item.
  • Confirm all trades 80% or over — a bulk action that only covers trades the AI was confident about.
  • Confirm all findings — a bulk action, but it skips exactly the items with the missing trades that the publish gate is waiting for, so you can confirm everything and still be blocked.
And thenThe draft summary has its own confirmation, and the reading view carries its own Confirm this finding. Six places in total, four of which are asking the same question at different levels.
What goes wrongYou press Confirm all findings, the count says everything is accepted, and publishing still refuses because a trade is missing. The number on the button and the number that decides publishing are not the same number.
2The proposal — one card, one button, one list of exceptionsPROPOSEDone screen change
The cardAt the top of the review screen, one line and one button: “222 findings drafted, 183 accepted, 39 need you” and Accept all 39. That single press settles status, grade, trade and wording for every item the AI was confident about, in one write.
The listUnder the button, only the exceptions — the items the AI was unsure about, the ones it could not assess, the ones where two items were found in one photo. Each has one press to settle, and the reason is written beside it in plain words.
The one ruleThe number on the button always equals what will actually be written, and the same number decides publishing. No more “confirmed everything, still blocked”.
Per itemOne button per finding instead of three: accepting a card settles its grade and its trade with the text. One undo, not three.

How it stays honest

  • Nothing is accepted that a person has not seen. The card states the number it is about to accept, before it is pressed, and the number is what gets written.
  • Not assessed items are never swept in. That rule exists today and stays: an item the AI could not read needs a human status, and one press cannot invent it.
  • The audit trail is unchanged. Every accepted finding still records who confirmed it and when, exactly as it does now — so the accountability the reports rely on is identical.
  • The AI’s confidence decides what goes in the quick pile. Three confidence scores are already stored per finding (wording, grade, trade). Below the line? It goes on the list, not in the bulk accept.
  • One undo. Published versions already freeze a copy, so a mis-press can never reach a published report.

Decision 2 — what does “accept all” include?

A. Accept everything the AI was confident about, list the rest. MY RECOMMENDATION
One press for the bulk, a short list of genuine judgement calls. On your current data that is roughly four in five findings settled in one press, with the doubtful fifth in front of you.
B. Accept absolutely everything, flag the doubtful ones afterwards. Fewest clicks, fastest, and it puts AI guesses into a report you sign. I would not recommend it on a document that goes to a client.
C. Keep per-item confirmation, but make it one press per item. A honest improvement, about half the work, but it does not fix the thing you actually described.

5Job 3 — the photo carrying six findings

You asked whether we could enlarge the photo, drop a numbered pin per item and reference the pin number. Yes — and the groundwork is closer than you would think.

1Why it repeats the picture todayMEASUREDroot cause
What you see25 of your photos carry more than one finding, and each finding draws its own copy of the whole photograph. A wall with four defects appears four times, full width, and nothing on the picture says which defect is which.
WhyThe code that draws a box round the relevant part of a photo reads the region saved on the finding’s photo link. That column has 0 rows. The AI’s region is saved on the finding instead, in a different column, and nothing reads it there.
SoThe feature is half built and switched off by a wiring mistake. The box, the overlay and the numbered marker tool all already exist. 105 of your findings are carrying a region nobody has ever seen.
2What I would build — your idea, plus four thingsPROPOSED5 parts
Your ideaOne photo, numbered pins. The photograph is shown once. Every item the AI found on it gets a numbered pin at the place it was read from, and the item list underneath reads “Pin 3 · F-017 · cracked render to north elevation”. The number is the same on screen, in the PDF, on the shared page and in the trade extract.
1. ZoomTapping a pin (or its item) zooms that patch full width. On paper, a small inset of the patch is printed beside the item instead of the whole photograph again — that is what takes pages out of the PDF.
2. Only your pinsIn a trade extract, that trade’s pins stay bright and the others dim. A subbie holding a 40-item report sees the three things that are his, on the right photo.
3. Pins you can moveThe region is the model’s estimate, so it will sometimes be in the wrong place. A person drags the pin; that becomes a saved markup on the photo, so the correction survives into the published version. This is why the drag ships with the pins, not after them.
4. Honest absence117 findings have no region at all. Where the AI gave nothing, no pin is invented — the item appears in the list without a marker, and the photo is shown once. I would rather show nothing than point confidently at the wrong place on a client report.

Is there anything better than pins? Two things.

  • Auto-crop beats a pin on paper. Because the AI already knows the patch, the PDF can print a tight crop of the defect instead of a wide site photo where the problem is 3% of the frame. A pin says where to look; a crop means you do not have to look. I would do both: pins on the photo, crops in the printed item list.
  • One photo instead of six is the real win. Not just tidier — it cuts the report by roughly a page per multi-item photograph and makes a snagging report readable on site, on a phone, in the rain.

Decision 3 — pins everywhere, or only where they earn their place?

A. Pins only where a photo carries more than one finding. MY RECOMMENDATION
A single-finding photo stays exactly as it looks today, big and clear. The new treatment appears only where the problem is. Nothing you like changes.
B. Pins on every photo. More consistent, and it costs you the large single photograph that reads best on a phone.
C. Region wiring first, pins later. Ship the wiring alone (a small job, half a session) so the boxes appear in reports tomorrow, and decide the pins after you have looked at a real one.
